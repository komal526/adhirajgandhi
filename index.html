PRACTICE AREA · CORPORATE

Corporate Matters

Cor­por­ate work is where advice has the most lever­age, because the decisions made at the start of a trans­ac­tion decide how well it sur­vives later. We act for private com­pan­ies, LLPs, pro­moters, investors and family-owned busi­nesses at every stage, from incor­por­a­tion and fun­drais­ing to restruc­tur­ing, sale and exit.

Mergers and Acquisitions

We advise buyers, sellers and pro­moters from first struc­tur­ing to com­ple­tion. The work begins with legal due dili­gence, which sur­faces title, con­trac­tual, reg­u­lat­ory and lit­ig­a­tion risks before they turn into price dis­putes. We then nego­ti­ate the term sheet and prepare or review the share or asset pur­chase agree­ment, with par­tic­u­lar care for rep­res­ent­a­tions, war­ranties, indem­nit­ies, con­di­tions pre­ced­ent and the alloc­a­tion of risk between signing and closing.

Shareholder and Joint Venture Arrangements

Where several parties own or run a busi­ness together, the share­hold­ers’ or joint venture agree­ment is the doc­u­ment that decides how choices are made and how dis­agree­ments end. We draft and review board and reserved-matter rights, trans­fer restric­tions, tag-along and drag-along pro­vi­sions, dead­lock mech­an­isms and exit routes, and we advise when dis­putes arise between pro­moters and investors.

Restructuring and Reorganisation

Busi­nesses change shape as they grow. Under­tak­ings are trans­ferred, groups are sim­pli­fied and own­er­ship passes between gen­er­a­tions. We advise on the legal route and the doc­u­ment­a­tion for busi­ness trans­fers, share reor­gan­isa­tions and arrange­ments within closely held and family com­pan­ies, together with the approvals each step requires.

Corporate Advisory and Compliance

On a con­tinu­ing basis, we advise boards and pro­moters on gov­ernance, dir­ect­ors’ duties, share­holder rights, reg­u­lat­ory com­pli­ance and the legal risk in com­mer­cial decisions. We also act in dis­putes among dir­ect­ors and share­hold­ers and in the recov­ery of dues owed to the company. The agree­ments that support this work are described in the Agree­ments section below.

Civil lit­ig­a­tion tests an agree­ment, a title or a right in open court, and it rewards pre­par­a­tion. We act for plaintiffs and defend­ants in civil and com­mer­cial courts across Haryana and the National Capital Region, with the aim of secur­ing the right interim pro­tec­tion early and a clear decree at the end.

We handle suits for pos­ses­sion, declar­a­tion of title and par­ti­tion among co-owners and family members, and dis­putes over bound­ar­ies, ease­ments and ten­an­cies, includ­ing evic­tion and rent matters.

PRACTICE AREA · NI ACT

NI Act Matters

A dis­hon­oured cheque is an offence under Section 138 of the Nego­ti­able Instru­ments Act, 1881, but the pro­ceed­ings turn on strict time limits and on the quality of the notice and the com­plaint. We act for com­plain­ants seeking to recover money and for accused persons defend­ing such com­plaints.

Demand Notice and Complaint

The law requires a written demand notice within thirty days of receiv­ing inform­a­tion of dis­hon­our, and a com­plaint within one month after the drawer’s fifteen-day period to pay has expired. We prepare the notice and the com­plaint with every ingredi­ent of the offence set out clearly, and we verify service and lim­it­a­tion before filing.

Interim Compensation and Appeal Deposit

A court may direct the accused to pay interim com­pens­a­tion of up to twenty per cent of the cheque amount under Section 143A, and an appel­late court may require a deposit of at least twenty per cent under Section 148. We advise on both, for the com­plain­ant and for the accused.

Defence and Trial

For accused persons, we examine the exist­ence of a legally enforce­able debt, the stat­utory pre­sump­tions under Sec­tions 118 and 139, service of the notice and the cir­cum­stances in which the cheque was signed and presen­ted, and we conduct cross-exam­in­a­tion accord­ingly.

Compounding, Settlement and Appeals

Most cheque cases can be com­poun­ded. We nego­ti­ate set­tle­ments and payment sched­ules and record them so that the pro­ceed­ings close prop­erly, and we appear in appeals and revi­sions.

What is a Section 138 NI Act case?

A Section 138 case arises when a cheque is dis­hon­oured for insuf­fi­cient funds or a similar reason. The payee must send a written demand notice within thirty days of learn­ing of the dis­hon­our, and a com­plaint may follow if payment is not made within fifteen days. The firm acts for both com­plain­ants and accused.

How do I file a cheque bounce case under Section 138 of the NI Act in Hisar?

Send a written legal notice within 30 days of learn­ing that the cheque was dis­hon­oured. The drawer then has 15 days to pay. If payment is not made, a com­plaint can be filed before the Judi­cial Magis­trate First Class (JMIC) within one month after those 15 days end. ADHIRAJ GANDHI & CO. drafts the notice, files the com­plaint and appears for com­plain­ants and accused.

What are BNS, BNSS and the IPC and CrPC?

The Bhar­atiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) and the Bhar­atiya Nagarik Surak­sha Sanhita (BNSS) replaced the Indian Penal Code (IPC) and the Code of Crim­inal Pro­ced­ure (CrPC) from 1 July 2024. Offences com­mit­ted before that date, and cases already pending, are gen­er­ally still dealt with under the earlier laws. The firm advises under both.

I received a court summons. What should I do?

A summons is the court’s written dir­ec­tion to appear on a fixed date. Do not ignore it, because non-appear­ance can lead to a warrant. Note the court, case number and date, gather your papers and speak to an advoc­ate before the hearing so that appear­ance, bail or a reply can be arranged.

How can I find my next date of hearing?

Use the case number or CNR number on the e-Courts ser­vices website for the dis­trict court, or check the court’s cause list. Keep the CNR number from your summons or order ready. Clients of the firm can also contact the office for the next date.

What are Section 498A IPC (Section 85 BNS) and the DV Act?

Section 498A of the IPC, now Section 85 of the BNS, deals with cruelty to a wife by her husband or his rel­at­ives. The Pro­tec­tion of Women from Domestic Viol­ence Act, 2005 is a sep­ar­ate civil remedy that can give pro­tec­tion, res­id­ence, main­ten­ance and custody orders. The firm acts for aggrieved women and for the persons accused or named as respond­ents.

How does divorce work for a marriage in Haryana?

Divorce can be by mutual consent, for example under Section 13B of the Hindu Mar­riage Act, 1955, or con­tested on the grounds the law allows. Mutual consent needs a joint peti­tion and a waiting period that courts can waive in suit­able cases. Main­ten­ance, custody and prop­erty terms are settled in the same pro­ceed­ings.

What is the difference between an appeal, a revision, a declaration suit and a recovery suit?

An appeal chal­lenges a judg­ment or decree before a higher court. A revi­sion asks a higher court to examine the leg­al­ity of an order. A suit for declar­a­tion (Section 34, Spe­cific Relief Act, 1963) asks the court to declare a legal right or status. A recov­ery suit seeks money due, includ­ing summary suits under Order XXXVII of the CPC. The firm files and defends all of these.
